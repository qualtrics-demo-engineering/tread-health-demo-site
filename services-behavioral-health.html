Behavioral Health

Compassionate counseling and psychiatric care for kids, teens, and adults, in person or by video. You do not have to have it all figured out to ask for help.

What we treat

Our team listens first. Together we figure out what kind of support will help, at a pace that feels right for you.

Counseling & therapy

One-on-one, couples, and family sessions with caring therapists.

Anxiety & depression care

Support and treatment for feeling worried, low, or stuck.

Child & teen mental health

Care that meets young people where they are, with parents involved as needed.

Stress, grief & life changes

A place to talk through the hard moments.

Medication management

Psychiatric providers who review and adjust medications with you.

Substance use support

Respectful care and referrals, with no judgment.

In a crisis or thinking about hurting yourself?

You are not alone. Call or text 988 (the Suicide & Crisis Lifeline in the U.S.) any time, day or night. If someone is in immediate danger, call 911.

What to expect

Asking for help is a strong first step, and you set the pace from there.

Reach out

Call or book online. A friendly intake coordinator will ask a few questions.

Meet your clinician

Your first visit is a conversation. There is no right way to start.

Build a plan together

You decide what support fits, from weekly talks to medication to a mix.

Keep going

Regular check-ins, by video or in person, help you keep making progress.

Who you will meet

Real people who will know your name.

TherapistsLicensed counselors and social workers.
Psychiatric providersEvaluation and medication management.
Care coordinatorsHelp with scheduling and next steps.

Common questions

Is what I share private?

Your visits are confidential. There are a few safety-related exceptions, and your clinician will explain them at your first visit.

Do I need a referral?

No. You can call or book online. Your insurance plan may have its own requirements, so it is worth checking.

Can my teen come on their own?

Teens can often be seen with a parent or guardian, and your clinician will talk with you both about what privacy looks like.

What does it cost?

Most insurance plans cover behavioral health. See our Accepted Insurance page, or ask us about financial assistance.
